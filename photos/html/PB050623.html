Full-spectrum conversion (535nm filter + channel swap)

Kodak Aerochrome is a legendary near-infrared film with surreal false colours. Unfortunately, it was discontinued in 2009, and the limited remaining supply is getting incredibly expensive. But what if I could make unlimited Aerochrome-like photos using my full-spectrum-modded digital camera?

Aerochrome's signature palette is red foliage and deep purple skies. It achieves this by mapping near-infrared in the scene to the red colour channel, actual red in the scene to the green colour channel, and green in the scene to the blue channel, meaning it takes more than just a simple colour filter to achieve the Aerochrome effect. However, it turns out that the right long-pass filter on a full-spectrum camera coupled with some channel tweaks in post is enough to replicate the colour shifts in a digital medium.

I started out with an orange filter, but that removed too much of the green colour information and made everything severely magenta-tinged. I tried again with a 535nm yellow filter, and this time I was able to get a pretty convincing result in bright sunlight. In the off chance you also have a full-spectrum digital camera and you want to try this for yourself, here are my Darktable color calibration module settings to get you started:

  • CAT adaptation: bypass
  • R: input B +2.000
  • G: input R +0.050, input B -0.100
  • B: input G +0.400, input B -0.900.
I also needed to bump up the exposure by about 2 stops. You might be able to get better results with a slightly shorter-wavelength filter to let through even more green (500-510nm ish), but personally I think I'm done buying filters for a while.
